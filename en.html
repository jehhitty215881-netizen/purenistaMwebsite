deux Doll series

In a line: the name is French for "two" — everything comes in pairs (twin visuals, gendered pairs, opposite dyes), and the item names even address "you".

  • Bases are French wordsChagrin (sorrow), Larme (tear), Âme (soul), Néant (void), Soleil (sun), Vérité (truth), Loin (far), Soirée, Rêve (dream), Perle (pearl).
  • Loves myth & classicsPsyche = Greek "soul" (symbol: the butterfly), Giselle = the ballet's ghost dancer, Marguerite = daisy, Minette = kitten.
  • Archaic Japanese in wafū poolstamayura (an instant), utakata (foam), the "fleeting sakura", kawahirako (archaic "butterfly"); shirotae & others are traditional color names.
  • C.D / F.D family thread#10 Charlotte D.'s Tears + Heritage Book → #11 F.D-Glass Key → #14 C.D-Papillon → #15 F.D-Papillon (likely one family's initials).
  • Why start at #4the intl server follows the JP timeline; #1–#3 ended before global synced, so global starts at #4 (2023/02).

What the character names mean Etymology

Eyes and hairs are mostly named after real given names, each carrying a meaning (only confirmed etymologies listed; game-coined names like Saintière are omitted).

Eye characters

  • Anne"grace" (Hebrew Hannah); Antoine= the French form of Anthony.
  • Louise / Louis"famous warrior" (Germanic Ludwig).
  • Camille"attendant at a rite" (Latin); Samuel"God has heard" (Hebrew).
  • Claris"bright, clear" (Latin clarus); Clement"merciful, mild" (Latin clemens).
  • Marguerite"pearl / daisy" (Greek margarites); Maximilian"the greatest" (Latin maximus).
  • Inez"pure, holy" (Spanish form of Agnes); Raul"wolf-counsel" (Spanish form of Ralph).
  • Giselle"pledge" (Germanic gisil); Psyche"soul, breath" (Greek).

  • Bénédicte"blessed" (Latin benedictus); Felix"happy, lucky" (Latin).

  • Sophie"wisdom" (Greek); Luca / Lucie / Lucian"light" (Latin lux).

Philosophy in the items The allusions in names

Many items borrow philosophical or mythological concepts as names — a metaphor in itself.

  • Simulacrum(melem #1) Baudrillard's theory — the copy replaces the real, the fake truer than truth; in the mirror room, "you in the mirror" is no longer you.
  • Logos(melem #2, "reason") Greek philosophy's "reason / word / cosmic order"; the EN version renders "reason" as this very term.
  • Néant(Deux #13) the core word of Sartre's Being and Nothingness — consciousness negating being; "oblivious Néant" = forgotten nothingness.
  • Chronos(Deux #13) the Greek personification of time (often conflated with the child-devouring Titan Cronus); dropped into the time pool.
  • Psyche(Deux #14, melem #1) Greek "soul, breath", symbolized by the butterfly, and the princess loved by Eros.
  • Aether(By(e) #3) Aristotle's fifth element, the heavenly substance — holding up Heavenly Garden's "heaven".
  • Zhuangzi's butterfly dream(#14/#15) Zhuangzi dreamed he was a butterfly and woke unsure whether he was a man who dreamed a butterfly or a butterfly dreaming a man — real vs illusory, echoing Psyche (soul = butterfly) and melem's mirror/simulacrum. (Interpretive.)
  • Mirror-flower, water-moon(melem #3) a Buddhist / East Asian idea: a flower in the mirror, a moon on the water — visible yet unattainable.
  • Impermanence · mono no aware(By(e) overall) the Japanese sense of the fleeting — soap bubbles, falling sakura, fading all say the same thing.
  • Zero(melem #1, Boundary Distortion → Zero) distortion pushed to zero: existence erased back into the void.

Myth & literary allusions Sources

The myth or classic behind a name — basic and deep, in one entry.

Omphale · Deux #6 male dress

In Greek myth the queen of Lydia. Heracles was sold to her as a slave, wearing women's clothes and doing women's work while she wore his lion skin — a myth of gender reversal, the perfect male counterpart to "Femme Fatale".

Giselle · Deux #14

The Romantic ballet: a peasant girl dies of a broken heart and becomes a Wili, a night-spirit that dances men to death. Yet in Act II she protects her faithless lover instead, holding on until the dawn bell strips the Wilis of power — forgiveness over revenge. "Giselle of the Void → The Heretical" draws on this ghost-dancer.

Psyche & Eros · Deux #14 / melem #1

Psyche is Greek for "soul, breath", symbolized by the butterfly, and the princess loved by Eros. Forbidden to see his face, she lit an oil lamp to look — a drop of oil woke him and he fled; after Aphrodite's trials she was made immortal and reunited with him. Hence the butterflies flooding #14.

Anemone & Adonis · By(e) #1

The anemone bloomed from Adonis's blood and Aphrodite's tears; its flower language is "brief love / forsaken hope" — the root of the pool's fading beauty.

Snow White · melem #4

"Mirror, Mirror on the Wall", the poison apple, the queen's jealousy; "the irreversible red fruit" = once bitten, no way back.

The passionflower · Deux #13

The "endless clock flower" = the passionflower. Clock-face shaped, its Japanese name means "clock plant", dropping it into the time pool; its Western name Passiflora was coined by missionaries who read its parts as the Passion of Christ — filament corona = crown of thorns, five anthers = five wounds, three stigmas = three nails. A double pun.

Orion · Deux #8

The giant huntsman Orion of Greek myth, set in the sky as the constellation Orion; the star motif fits the "star/night" blue-rose pool.

Camille / Camilla · the warrior in the name

The name means "attendant at a rite", but in Virgil's Aeneid Camilla is a virgin warrior devoted to Diana, so swift she could skim over grain and water without a trace — echoing the fierce "femme fatale" Camille.

Marguerite · also Faust's tragic heroine

Beyond "pearl / daisy", Marguerite (Gretchen) is the tragic heroine of Goethe's Faust — seduced, ruined, then redeemed. (Connection is interpretive.)

Q: Do brand pools ever rerun? Brands

A: None has rerun so far. Once a pool closes, trading is the only route — and older years are generally more prized. (Per official announcements.)

Q: When do dyes expire? Dyeing

A: It differs by brand — Deux uses a rolling four-season window (at #16, pools #13–#16 are dyeable); melem is current-pool only and candles expire the moment the pool ends; By(e) uses keys, so dyeing last season needs a pull in the current one. See "Brand dyeing".

Q: Can I dye without owning the original? Dyeing

A: Deux requires owning that item's original color; melem's mirror rooms do not require the matching outfit.

Q: How do I tell an original from a dye? How-to

A: The color worn in the big preview at the top is the original; the other thumbnails in the grid are dyeable versions. Originals are usually saturated or pure tones (white, silver, black, deep red, pink).

Q: If I switch platforms (iOS ⇄ Android), will my stars disappear? How-to

A: No, they won't disappear — stars just aren't shared between platforms. Switch back to your original platform and your stars will still be there.

Q: How do I hide / show my duplicate list? How-to

A: Top-right main menu → Wish List · Duplicate List → "public setting" at the top right of the duplicate list.

Q: How do I set whether new duplicates are auto-published? How-to

A: Top-right main menu → Settings → Auto-set duplicate list.

Q: About "gift trading" / "stars-for-items" trading? Info

A: These are trading activities players developed privately — not part of official gameplay; all carry risk, proceed at your own discretion.

Q: How do I self-fix bugs (crashes, missing head, art-photo screen stuck, etc.)? How-to

A: Settings (gear icon) → Clear cache. It won't delete your game data and solves most issues. ⚠️ Not guaranteed to work every time — contact official support if the problem persists.

Q: How do I issue a transfer (inheritance) code? How-to

A: Log in ➜ main menu ➜ Settings ➜ Issue transfer code.
※ Tip: if possible, issue a fresh code regularly and keep a screenshot — it may help you recover your account if you ever lose access.
