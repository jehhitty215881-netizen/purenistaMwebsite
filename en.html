What decides value Read the market

A lot of people only know "BRAND #4 is expensive" without knowing why. These 5 factors decide where an item actually lands — the more stars, the bigger the pull on the ratio:

Age (the older, the fewer people have it)★★★★

How to decline politely No hard feelings

If you get an offer you don't want, leaving them on read tends to sting. Try something like:

  • Give a reason"Sorry, I want to keep this one / I've already got a trade lined up with someone else — thanks for asking though!"
  • Don't put them downavoid anything like "yours isn't good enough" — just saying "not looking for a trade right now" is enough.
  • Leave the door openif it's really just that the ratio isn't to your liking, try proposing a different arrangement, or go with "sorry, I'd rather not trade this way" instead of a flat no.

How to negotiate Comfortable for both sides

  • Ask what they think first"what ratio were you thinking?" finds middle ground faster than opening high yourself.
  • Use "the going rate," not "I feel like""stuff expired a year-plus usually goes around 1:2–3 in this community" lands better than "I feel like it's worth more" — and causes fewer arguments.
  • Move a little at a timestep toward each other from your opening offers instead of caving all at once — conceding too fast makes people wonder if your opener was made up.
  • Shelve it if you can't agree"let's both think it over and circle back if we land on something" beats forcing it or getting heated.

What's a "door-kick opener" A testing lowball

A door-kick opener means deliberately opening way below the going rate, to test whether you know the market and how easy you are to push around. Not every low offer is malicious, but common tells:

  • The opener sits clearly below the ranges listed in "Old items · by expiry time" or "Dye item" tables.
  • They jump up readily once you point out the going rate — a sign they knew the rate all along and were just probing.
  • Openers like "just asking, no worries if not" that lower the pressure on you to say no.

No need to get upset — just quote a ratio you're comfortable with. If they genuinely want the trade, they'll usually come back to a reasonable range.

Phrases that tend to backfire Watch your wording

  • "Plenty of people are selling this anyway"implies their item isn't worth much — even if true, it stings.
  • "Fine, forget it then"turns a negotiation into a threat and usually shuts the conversation down entirely.
  • "Everyone else calculates it this way"leaning on a vague "everyone" without showing an actual rate is less convincing, not more.
  • A cold "so?" after leaving them on readeven if the content is fine, the tone alone can make people feel disrespected and end the trade.

When to be extra careful This is pattern-recognition, not a blacklist

The point here isn't to suspect everyone — it's a handful of common scam or pitfall patterns. If several of these show up together, slow down.

  • Constantly rushing the tradea normal trader lets you double-check; unusual pressure is often about getting you to send before you look closely.
  • Refusing to state the ratio openlyinsisting on discussing it privately with no screenshots leaves you with no record if things go wrong later.
  • Terms keep changingthe agreed ratio, order, or contents shift right up to the last second, wearing down your attention.
  • Asking you to send first"show me you're serious first" or "I got scammed before so you go first" — a real trade should be simultaneous or witnessed by a trusted third party; sending first, alone, carries the most risk.
  • "I'll send it back later"they take your item and stall on sending theirs with excuses — the longer it drags on, the harder it is to recover.

Screenshot the agreed terms and ratio before trading. Try to trade simultaneously where possible, and for higher-value trades, ask a trusted third party to witness.

Top 10 rookie mistakes Read this before your first trade

  • 1. Trading PURE for PRM at 1:1PURE and PRM items cost different amounts to obtain — 1:1 usually shortchanges whoever's giving up the PRM item.
  • 2. Not knowing LUCKYBAG counts as PURELUCKYBAG items are pulled with free stars, so they're priced like PURE items in a trade — not opened as if they were PRM.
  • 3. Not knowing BRAND baskets never rerunonce a BRAND basket ends it's retired for good — the only way in is trading, and the older it is, the more it's usually worth. Regular-basket logic doesn't apply here.
  • 4. Confusing "basket ended" with "dye expired""dye expired" isn't about how long ago the basket closed — it's about whether the item can still be dyed. The two price very differently (see the "Dye item" table).
  • 5. Falling for the word "popular"a seller calling their item popular doesn't make it so — check community discussion or circulation yourself instead of taking their word for it.
  • 6. Trading same rarity, different type, at face valuea free SR and a paid SR aren't worth the same — confirm both sides are the same type before converting.
  • 7. No screenshotsverbal agreements turn into "he said, she said" — screenshot the terms before you trade.
  • 8. Sending firstwhatever the reason given, whoever sends first always carries more risk — trade simultaneously whenever you can.
  • 9. Treating a door-kick opener as the going ratea first offer isn't automatically fair — check it against tables like "Old items · by expiry time" before accepting.
  • 10. Letting a failed negotiation turn uglyratios not lining up is normal — "let me think about it" leaves the door open for a future trade far better than a snippy exchange does.

Q: Do brand baskets ever rerun? Brands

A: None has rerun so far. Once a basket closes, trading is the only route — and older years are generally more prized. (Per official announcements.)

Q: When do dyes expire? Dyeing

A: It differs by brand — Deux uses a rolling four-season window (at #16, baskets #13–#16 are dyeable); melem is current-basket only and candles expire the moment the basket ends; By(e) uses keys, so dyeing last season needs a pull in the current one. See "Brand dyeing".

Q: Can I dye without owning the original? Dyeing

A: Deux requires owning that item's original color; melem's mirror rooms do not require the matching outfit.

Q: How do I tell an original from a dye? How-to

A: The color worn in the big preview at the top is the original; the other thumbnails in the grid are dyeable versions. Originals are usually saturated or pure tones (white, silver, black, deep red, pink).

Q: When is the next brand basket? Prediction

A: Based on past patterns, Deux #16 is expected on 2026/08/01; melem is fixed to Nov 1 and By(e) to May. Full reasoning under "Schedule". For reference only — official announcements rule.

Q: If I switch platforms (iOS ⇄ Android), will my stars disappear? How-to

A: No, they won't disappear — stars just aren't shared between platforms. Switch back to your original platform and your stars will still be there.

Q: How do I hide / show my duplicate list? How-to

A: Top-right main menu → Wish List · Duplicate List → "public setting" at the top right of the duplicate list.

Q: How do I set whether new duplicates are auto-published? How-to

A: Top-right main menu → Settings → Auto-set duplicate list.

Q: About "gift trading" / "stars-for-items" trading? Info

A: These are trading activities players developed privately — not part of official gameplay; all carry risk, proceed at your own discretion.

Q: How do I self-fix bugs (crashes, missing head, art-photo screen stuck, etc.)? How-to

A: Settings (gear icon) → Clear cache. It won't delete your game data and solves most issues. ⚠️ Not guaranteed to work every time — contact official support if the problem persists.

Q: Why wasn't a particular composition detected? Artwork check

A: Detection needs something measurable in the picture — a C-curve needs a continuous arc, a radial needs several lines converging on one point. Not being detected does not mean the composition is bad; it means that piece did not use that device.

Version 5.3.1 art-check judgement fixes

Fixed: art-check false positives (cross-checked on real plates)

  • Warm / cool label: low-chroma grey and silver illustrations were often tagged "warm" because only high-saturation hues voted, so a few skin or accent pixels pushed the ratio over the line while cool greys abstained. Near-neutral pixels now vote via the Lab blue–yellow axis, low-chroma frames lean on measured Lab, and the "warm" DNA line requires both ratio and Lab to agree.
  • Unity and variety: restrained mono, cinematic and Morandi plates score low on variety by design; the geometric mean then looked like a fault. Low-chroma / high-harmony work no longer lists "unity and variety not differentiated" as a defect — that is the style, not an error.
  • Scattered focus: soft light, full moons and halos spread brightness so area-holding-half-the-weight looked "unfocused". When a face is detected, or a centre / radial read is strong, the hard "no landing point" penalty is skipped.
  • Uneven negative space: branch frames, dark mass and magazine title bands are often deliberate asymmetry. In design mode with text, vertical gaps are no longer a hard fault; in illustration mode with a centre / radial pattern, mild left–right imbalance is heavily down-weighted.
  • Black screen / visionState: a leftover undeclared variable could blank the result panel; it is now initialised, and errors draw text instead of a black canvas.
  • Aesthetics sub-tabs unclickable: listeners bound before lazy content injection never attached. Click handling is now document-level delegation so sub-tabs work after hydrate.
